AI Flower: scoring and interpretation

AI Flower shows where you see promise, where you see harm, and where you see both. It also asks how much change you expect and how you feel about it.

“Stretch” means agreeing with both the benefit and harm statement about the same issue. You can hold both views without feeling torn. Mixed feelings, inner conflict and difficulty deciding are asked about separately.

The result describes your answers at this moment. It is not a personality diagnosis.

Consciousness, corporate trust, use frequency, consent, precaution and regulation do not carry hidden optimism or pessimism points. Questions about existential risk are shown as individual answers because they concern longer-term possibilities, rather than the five-year effects covered by the benefit and harm summaries.

1. Benefits and harms: what the scores mean

Benefits and harms each have their own score. Agreeing with a harm statement does not reduce your benefit score, or vice versa.

Each answer has a value from 0 to 4. The score is the average of your answered statements, multiplied by 25:

Score = average answer × 25
ScoreAverage response
0Strongly disagree
25Disagree
50Neither agree nor disagree
75Agree
100Strongly agree

These numbers describe agreement with the statements. They are not probabilities, percentages of people or rankings against other respondents.

A score of 50 can come from neutral answers throughout, or from strong agreement on some issues and strong disagreement on others. The answer breakdown shows which is the case.

Each of the seven issues has equal weight. A score appears once at least four of its seven statements have a substantive answer. The number answered is shown alongside it. “Not sure / skip” and unanswered statements are left out; the neutral answer counts as 2. Scores are rounded to the nearest whole number, with halves rounded up.

The four-answer minimum is a practical coverage rule, not a validated threshold. Scores based on different sets of answered issues may not be directly comparable.

2. How much change you expect

Three questions ask how much AI will change your learning, teaching or work; education; and society over the next five years. They ask about the amount of change, whether you welcome it or not.

AnswerValue on the 0–100 scale
None0

Moderate50

Fundamental100

The overall score is the average across all three settings. It appears only when all three questions have substantive answers. Your answers for each setting are also shown, so a small expected change in your own day does not hide a large expected change in society.

AI use frequency and beliefs about artificial general intelligence do not affect this score.

3. Where you see both benefit and harm

Seven issues have a matching benefit and harm statement. A topic counts as “both/and” when you answer Agree or Strongly agree to both.

Your answers within an issueHow it is described
Agree or strongly agree with both statementsYou see both
Agree or strongly agree only with the benefit statementYou see benefit
Agree or strongly agree only with the harm statementYou see harm
Neither statement receives agreementNeither view is endorsed
Either answer is missing or Not sure / skipIncomplete pair

“Neither endorsed” includes neutral and disagreeing answers. It does not mean you are indifferent. Your original ratings remain visible.

The result gives a count, such as:

You agreed with both the benefit and harm statement in 5 of 7 comparable topics.

This count appears once at least four pairs are complete. It includes only topics where both answers are known. Seeing benefit in healthcare and harm in jobs does not count as seeing both within either issue.

The equivalent percentage is the number of both/and topics divided by the number of complete pairs, multiplied by 100. The count is shown because it makes the coverage clearer. A change from Neutral to Agree can change that count; it does not establish a sudden change in your feelings. This is a description of the chosen answers, not a validated ambivalence scale or a basis for ranking people.

4. How it feels to you

The final three questions ask about mixed feelings, inner conflict and difficulty deciding. Each answer is shown on its own, from Not at all to Very much. Missing answers stay unscored.

These are different experiences. You may see benefits and harms clearly while feeling settled about your position. You may also feel torn for reasons the seven paired issues do not capture.

Only your direct answer about inner conflict supports a statement that you feel torn. The both/and count does not supply that answer.

5. Reading your flower and card

Each of the seven petals represents one issue: creative work, jobs, information and truth, healthcare, learning and teaching, who benefits, or the environment.

FeatureWhat it shows
Green through yellow to redYour benefit rating compared with your harm rating. Green leans towards benefit; red towards harm; yellow means equal ratings.
Petal lengthHow strongly you agree with either statement. Stronger agreement makes a longer petal.
Smooth or jagged textureAgreement with both statements. Agreeing with both adds texture; strongly agreeing with both adds deeper texture.
Rising or drooping tipsThe same benefit–harm balance as the colour: green rises, red droops.
Short, smooth petalNeither statement received agreement.
Grey petalOne or both answers are missing or Not sure / skip.
StemDecoration. It carries no score.

Yellow can mean strong agreement with both statements, neutrality on both, or disagreement with both. Petal length, texture and the answer readout distinguish these cases. Jaggedness shows both/and in your answers; it does not show how distressed or conflicted you feel.

How the petal is calculated

Colour follows the difference between the two original 0–4 ratings: (benefit − harm) ÷ 4. Equal ratings produce yellow.

For length and texture, each rating becomes 0 for Strongly disagree, Disagree or Neutral; 0.5 for Agree; and 1 for Strongly agree. Length follows the larger of these two values. Texture follows the smaller, so it increases only when both statements receive agreement. These values set the drawing, not a new psychological score.

Petal width and the stem are decorative. The rise or droop repeats the colour signal. Turning, zooming and the Visual relief control change your view of the flower, not your answers. Its area, volume and overall colour are not scored.

7. Research basis and limits

GAAIS, Schepman & Rodway: supports distinguishing positive and negative AI attitudes. The original scale has initial validation and a later confirmatory study. This questionnaire uses new topic statements and is not the GAAIS. Its validation does not transfer to our wording or indices.

  • Initial study: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7231759/
  • Confirmatory study: https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2022.2085400

Evaluative Space Grid, Larsen and colleagues: supports representing positive and negative evaluations separately. Our matched topic counts are a descriptive summary, not the published grid or a validated implementation of it.

  • https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930801994054

Priester & Petty: distinguishes positive/negative bases of attitudes from subjective ambivalence. Our reflection items are adapted/original prompts inspired by that work. Their wording, response range and separate reporting need evaluation.

  • https://richardepetty.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/1996-jpsp-priesterpetty.pdf

Paradox Mindset Inventory: addresses a broader disposition towards tensions. It is not used here: agreeing with both sides of a few AI statements is insufficient to infer someone's paradox mindset.

  • https://paradox.lerner.udel.edu/moreinformation.php

No copied full instrument, psychological diagnosis, population norms, calibrated match probabilities or validated archetypes are included.
